# Go-to-Market Strategy

## 1. The fundamental GTM truth for this market

**You cannot acquire supply and demand at the same time.** A marketplace that launches wide on both
sides launches empty on both sides. Pick one side to solve first.

**Solve supply first — but only supply that has demand attached.** That is what Phase 0's concierge
work is for: you arrive at a distributor not with a pitch but with a purchase order.

## 2. Beachhead selection

Score each candidate beachhead 1–5 on:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Density** | How many qualifying plants within a 150 km radius? (Logistics cost per drop decides your margin) |
| **Repetition** | Do they buy weekly? |
| **Fragmentation** | Are they served by many small distributors rather than one dominant one? |
| **Pain** | Have they been burned recently — a supply outage, a price shock, a failed audit? |
| **Reachability** | Is there one trade association, cluster, or WhatsApp group that reaches all of them? |
| **Payment discipline** | Do they pay in 30 days or 180? |

**Recommended first beachhead:** *water treatment & utility chemicals (S23) for mid-size industrial
plants and municipalities within one industrial cluster.*

Why: highest purchase frequency, highest standardisation (specs are well defined), recurring
contracts, measurable outcomes (the buyer can see whether the chemical worked), and the buyer
already tracks consumption — so they will notice a price change. It is the easiest place to prove
value and the easiest place to expand from, because the same trucks can carry cleaning chemicals
(S24) and consumables (S45–S48).

**Recommended second beachhead:** *detergent & liquid-washing manufacturers* — deeper chemistry
(S20, S21, S12, S13.4), higher order values, and a well-defined line (S43) you can sell as a bundle.

## 3. The supply-side motion

| Motion | Tactic |
|---|---|
| **Anchor suppliers** | Sign 5–8 distributors who already serve your beachhead. Give them: zero listing fee for 12 months, first-mover badge, guaranteed order flow from your pilot plants, and a free compliance-document upgrade |
| **The distributor's real problem** | They are not short of buyers — they are short of *new* buyers and long of *dead stock*. Offer them S65/S66 (surplus & off-spec) as a channel to clear inventory. That is a genuine pain you solve for free |
| **Producers** | Approach producers for a "direct-to-plant" programme where you handle the compliance documentation and last-mile they cannot economically do for small buyers |
| **Onboarding cost** | You must do the data entry. Ask for their price list in any format — Excel, PDF, photo of a whiteboard — and your team (later, an LLM-assisted pipeline) normalises it into the taxonomy. Never make the supplier do your data work |

## 4. The demand-side motion

1. **The audit trigger.** The single most reliable sales trigger in this market is an upcoming
   customer or regulatory audit. Lead with the compliance pack, not the price. *"We will have your
   full SDS set, current revisions, and DG declarations exportable in one click before your audit."*
   Then mention the price index as a second reason.
2. **The second-source offer.** Every plant manager knows they should have a second supplier and
   almost none do, because qualification is painful. Sell qualification as a service: free sample,
   spec comparison against their incumbent, trial-batch support, documented result. This is the
   offer that gets you in the door without a price war.
3. **The line bundle.** For a new plant or a line upgrade, quote the whole line — reactor, dosing
   pumps, IBCs, chemicals, PPE, filter bags — as one package with one delivery schedule and one
   invoice. Nobody else can do this. It is also your highest-value order type.
4. **Trade associations and clusters.** One presentation to a regional manufacturers' association
   reaches 200 plants. This is the cheapest B2B channel that exists in this sector.
5. **Content that ranks.** Programmatic, genuinely useful pages: *"Caustic soda flakes 98% —
   specification, storage class SC-2, UN 1823, handling and segregation"*, *"How to declare a
   Class 8 shipment"*, *"LABSA 90% vs SLES 70% — what to specify for liquid detergent"*. Chemical
   search terms have low volume and very high intent, and nobody writes good pages for them.

## 5. Retention — where the business actually is

Acquisition gets you the first order. These keep the account:

| Lever | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **The document vault** | Once three years of SDS revisions, CoAs and DG declarations live in your system, migrating away is a genuine loss. This is your strongest lock-in and it costs you almost nothing |
| **Contract price sheets with index linkage** | Annual contracts mean the buyer stops shopping every order |
| **Consumables replenishment** | Auto-suggest reorder when their filter-bag or glove consumption pattern says they are running low |
| **The warehouse segregation plan** | Their EHS lead builds their safety plan on your output |
| **ERP punchout (Phase 3)** | Once you are in their purchasing system, replacing you is a project |
| **Credit terms** | Realistically, this is the #1 reason a small plant stays with a distributor. Partner on financing early |

## 6. Pricing the platform to suppliers

| Tier | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Listed (T1)** | Free | Directory entry, enquiry routing, **no transactions** |
| **Verified (T2)** | Free → then $50/mo | Full verification, transactions enabled, escrow |
| **Verified+ (T3)** | $200–500/mo | Site-verified badge, priority in ranking, RFQ alerts before others, analytics dashboard, dedicated onboarding |
| **Anchor partner** | Negotiated | Co-marketing, exclusive segment rights in a region, index participation |

**Rule:** never charge to be listed, only to be prioritised and enabled. Charging for listing fills
your marketplace with whoever has a marketing budget rather than whoever has the best supply.

## 7. Launch sequence, concretely

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| −8 to −4 | 8 anchor suppliers signed and their catalogues loaded by your team |
| −4 to −2 | 10 pilot plants onboarded with their compliance documents already in the vault (a gift, not a chore) |
| −2 | Soft launch: catalogue live, transactions manual behind the scenes |
| 0 | **Public launch of one segment only** (S23). Announce the price index for 5 SKUs |
| +2 | First trade-association presentation |
| +4 | Publish the first weekly price index — this is your PR asset and your credibility proof |
| +8 | Add S24 (cleaning chemicals) and S45–S48 (consumables) |
| +12 | Turn on supplier subscriptions |

## 8. Metrics that tell you the GTM is working

- **RFQ fill rate ≥70%** — if you cannot find 3 qualified quotes per line, your supply side is thin
- **First-order-to-second-order interval <45 days** — if buyers take longer, you are selling
  one-off transactions, not a procurement relationship
- **Zero-price-request share** — the fraction of orders placed from a contract price sheet without
  a new quote. Rising = trust is forming
- **Supplier-initiated listings/month** — if suppliers start adding SKUs unprompted, supply is
  pulling rather than being pushed
